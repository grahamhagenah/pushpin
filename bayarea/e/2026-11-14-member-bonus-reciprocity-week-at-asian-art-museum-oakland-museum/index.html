For over six decades, pioneering artist Ha Chong-Hyun (b. 1935) has explored what painting becomes when freed from all existing conventions. Ha’s work prizes the interaction of his materials: using pressure, gravity, and tools ranging from torches to everyday utensils, he transforms the surfaces of canvases up to eight feet tall into a treasure trove of texture and physicality. His first museum exhibition in North America, Ha Chong-Hyun: Retrospective offers an unprecedented look at the creative evolution of one of Korea’s most influential contemporary artists.

From his birth as a precious egg to his decision to serve as the vehicle of the Hindu god Vishnu, the divine adventures of Garuda come to life in this evocative exhibition. Part-bird, part-man, and the epitome of loyalty and courage, Garuda holds profound significance not only as a fierce warrior, but as the archetype of a deeply devoted son.

Featuring wooden sculptures and traditional paintings from the late 19th and early 20th centuries, the exhibition traces Garuda’s legendary exploits, capturing both the artistic mastery of Balinese creators and the enduring spiritual resonance of a protector deity who remains central to Indonesian culture today.

Beyond the ancient temples of Angkor lies a deeply compelling contemporary art scene. This exhibition spotlights works by Sopheap Pich and Leang Seckon, two of Cambodia’s most internationally prominent artists. Born in the 1970s, both lived through the Khmer Rouge genocide and emerged in the early 2000s as key figures in the country’s artistic revival.
