Lowell Lecture Series: Sarah Botstein, Co-Director of The American Revolution from Florentine Films

Boston Public Library · Tue, Sep 22 · 6pm

Join us in person or over Zoom Webinar as the Boston Public Library welcomes Sarah Botstein as a speaker for the Lowell Lecture Series. As a filmmaker who has worked closely with Ken Burns for over 30 years as a part of Florentine Films, Sarah will talk about her latest film, PBS's The American Revolution, historical filmmaking, and the revolutionary techniques that Florentine Films uses to bring history to life.

Botstein will be in conversation with Imari Paris Jeffries from Embrace Boston and the program will be introduced by BPL President David Leonard.

After the main program, in-person audience members can meet the speaker in the Connector Space located just outside of Rabb Hall.

This program will happen in-person and over Zoom Webinar. We plan to open doors twenty minutes in advance for our in-person audience.

Sarah Botstein has worked with Ken Burns for nearly 30 years and produced some of his most popular and acclaimed documentaries on PBS. Most recently, she produced and co-directed The American Revolution. Her previous work includes The U.S. and the Holocaust, Hemingway, College Behind Bars, The Vietnam War, Prohibition, The War, and Jazz.

Botstein also works closely with PBS LearningMedia to develop educational materials as part of the Ken Burns Classroom, and she was an original contributor to Ken Burns's UNUM.

Botstein is now working on a three-part series about Lyndon B. Johnson and the Great Society.

Sarah Botstein is joined by Imari Paris Jeffries, CEO of Embrace Boston.

Imari Paris Jeffries, Ph.D., is President and CEO of Embrace, an arts, culture, and community organization reshaping public memory as public policy and advancing belonging through justice. His work begins with a simple premise: cities are shaped not only by policy and economics, but by story, monument, silence, and who is remembered.

Under his leadership, Embrace has grown into a civic force committed to centering Black life in public space. The Embrace Monument on Boston Common honoring Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. and Coretta Scott King stands as a declaration that love, struggle, and Black life belong at the heart of the city's landscape. What began as a monument has evolved into a movement, paired with institutional commitments designed to shift culture in lasting ways.
