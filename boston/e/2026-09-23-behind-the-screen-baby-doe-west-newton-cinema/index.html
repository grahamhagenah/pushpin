Behind the Screen: Baby Doe

West Newton Cinema · Wed, Sep 23 · 6:30pm

Thirty years ago, Gail Ritchey, a young woman from a conservative Christian community in rural Ohio, had a stillborn birth alone and left the evidence in the woods. Now a devoted mother of three, her quiet suburban life is shattered when DNA evidence links her to the infamous cold case of “Geauga’s Child,” leading to her arrest for murder. Facing a possible life sentence, Gail must confront the weight of societal and religious pressures—and her own long-buried shame—to ﬁght for her freedom.

Following the screening, join a conversation with director Jessica Earnshaw and WBUR host Deborah Becker on the making of the film and the questions it raises about maternal mental health, the criminal justice system, and the untold stories of women in crisis.

Jessica Earnshaw is an award-winning documentary filmmaker and photojournalist whose work focuses on criminal justice, family, and the lives of women. Her first feature, JACINTA, won the Albert Maysles Best New Documentary Director Award at the 2020 Tribeca Film Festival and received two Cinema Eye Honors nominations. Her photography has appeared in National Geographic, The New York Times, The Wall Street Journal, and NPR. She was named one of DOC NYC's "40 Under 40" in 2020.

Deborah Becker is a senior correspondent and host at WBUR. Her reporting focuses on mental health, criminal justice and education, helping to produce and report for various WBUR special projects. Deb has received numerous awards for her hosting, newscasts, reporting and investigative reporting from the Radio Television Digital News Association (RTNDA), Public Radio News Directors Incorporated, National Education Writers Association, Associated Press, Corporation for Public Broadcasting, National Alliance on Mental Illness, Parent/Professional Advocacy League and United Press International. She has also completed several fellowships on addiction, mental health, juvenile justice and journalism and the law.
