The History of Spiritualism in Boston

Charlestown Library · Thu, Oct 15 · 6pm

In the mid-1800s, a nationwide fascination with the afterlife took hold, deeply impacting Boston and its surrounding communities. Join local author and professional medium Mary Lee Trettenero for a fascinating talk on the history of the Spiritualism movement in Boston.

We’ll start with its beginnings in 1848, when the Fox sisters in New York claimed to communicate with spirits, sparking a nationwide interest.

We’ll explain what Spiritualists believe—that life continues after death, that the living can communicate with those who have passed on, and that these messages can offer comfort and guidance.

The lecture will highlight important Boston figures, including Margery Crandon, the famous and controversial local medium; Luther Colby, who published The Banner of Light, the most important newspaper of the Spiritualist movement; Emma Hardinge Britten, a well-known British medium who frequently gave demonstrations in Boston and shaped modern Spiritualism; and Adin Ballou, a reformer who connected Spiritualist ideas with social change. We’ll also touch on local séance gatherings, Sir Arthur Conan Doyle (author of the Sherlock Holmes novels and a prominent pioneer in the Spiritualist movement), and Harry Houdini, who investigated fraud in the mediumship movement.

This discussion highlights key Spiritualist figures in Victorian Charlestown and their contributions to the movement’s growth. Finally, I will talk about the Spiritualist churches active in the metro Boston area today.
