Arthur Miller Lecture on Science and Ethics

MIT · Mon, Oct 26 · 4pm

The Program in Science, Technology, and Society invites you to the annual Arthur Miller Lecture in Science and Ethics on Monday, October 26th from 4:00-5:30 pm in the MIT Welcome Center, for a panel lecture on the use of chatbots in children's toys. The panel features Professor Mimi Ito, Director of the Connected Learning Lab, Professor Sherry Turkle, founding director of the MIT Initiative on Technology and Self, and Mike Rizkalla, CEO & Founder of Snorble, a visionary leader in AI, consumer robotics, humanoid robotics, and children's technology.

2027 Arthur Miller Lecture on Science and Ethics There are many views surrounding the advances in children's toys with artificial intelligence capabilities. From a sociological standpoint, some experts believe children should not be the consumers of relational AI, programs that pretend to have a self, capable of humanlike connection. Chatbots draw us into a game of pretend — and for some, pretend empathy becomes empathy enough. While the seductions of pretend empathy are dangerous for all of us, children have no defense; they are first-time learners.

Others in the field view the advent of these new computers, or robots and physical AI, as a chance to change the outcomes we build for ourselves and to realign incentives. Joy, laughter, and love can be something different to everyone. To a child, it is having someone to play with. To a parent, it is five minutes where nothing is going wrong. To a grandparent, it is seeing their family happy.

Embodied AI is a blank canvas. A new mix of cameras, sensors, voice, character, and context gives us a whole new toolkit to do good things, to help people in ways that were never available before. We need to stop running in the other direction and embrace the tech before it becomes weaponized. We need smart, idealistic people leading what comes next, and that is where the smart money should be.

Mimi Ito is a cultural anthropologist, learning scientist, entrepreneur, and an advocate for connected learning—learning that is equity-oriented, centered on youth interest, and socially connected. Her work decodes digital youth culture for parents and educators, offering ways to tap interests and digital media to fuel learning that is engaging, relevant, and social.
