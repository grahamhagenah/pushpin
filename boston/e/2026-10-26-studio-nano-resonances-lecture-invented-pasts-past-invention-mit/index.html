Why do stories about technological inventions survive even when historical evidence proves them wrong? This talk explores the persistence of fabricated pasts through the remarkable case of Flavio Gioia, the alleged medieval inventor of the navigational compass. Created by a sixteenth-century translation mistake, Gioia never existed, yet he lived a remarkably long life on the printed page and beyond. For centuries, he figured in histories of technology and accounts of the making of the modern world. Following Gioia across texts, images, and monuments, the talk examines what the persistence of technological myths reveals about the role of inventions in narratives of modernity.

Paola Bertucci is a professor in the Department of History and in the History of Science and Medicine Program at Yale University. Her work has focused broadly on science, technology, and medicine in seventeenth- and eighteenth-century Europe, and more recently on fabricates realities and technologies of information and disinformation before the digital age.

She is the author of Artisanal Enlightenment: Science and the Mechanical Arts in Old Regime France (Yale University Press, 2017), winner of the 2019 Louis Gottschalk from the American Society for Eighteenth-Century Studies, and In the Land of Marvels. Science, Fabricated Realities, and Industrial Espionage in the Age of the Grand Tour (Johns Hopkins University Press, 2023), winner of 2025 Paul Bunge prize for best book on the history of scientific instruments.

Professor Bertucci is eager to bring innovative scholarly perspectives to broader audiences through museum exhibitions. She serves as the Curator-in-charge of the History of Science and Technology Division of the Yale Peabody Museum, where she co-curated the first permanent gallery at Yale dedicated to the History of Science and Technology.
