2026 Druker Award Lecture: Can Architecture Change A City?

Boston Public Library · Sat, Nov 7 · 2pm

Please join us in person or online over Zoom Webinar for the 2026 Druker Award presentation and talk with KPMB Architects’ Marianne McKenna and Paulo Rocha.

This 2026 Druker Award lecture explores how cities are continually reshaped, with KPMB Architects’ Marianne McKenna and Paulo Rocha examining Boston’s evolution and the story behind Boston University’s Duan Family Center for Computing & Data Sciences. Through the lens of this landmark project, they’ll consider how the design process and partnership can create lasting social, environmental, and economic impact. They’ll also look to the future of architecture in Boston.

Marianne McKenna, Founding Partner, KPMB Architects Marianne McKenna, the 2025 RAIC Gold Medalist, is a founding partner of KPMB Architects and an Officer of the Order of Canada. She is internationally known for her leadership on major cultural landmarks, including the Royal Conservatory of Music’s TELUS Centre for Performance and Learning and Koerner Hall, the revitalization of Toronto’s historic Massey Hall, the renewal of Orchestra Hall in Minneapolis, and the Jenny Belzberg Theatre at the Banff Centre for Arts and Creativity. Her approach balances deep respect for architectural legacy with contemporary interventions that support new uses, improved performance, and broader public access. She is a passionate advocate for design excellence, sustainability, and community‑building through architecture.

Paulo Rocha is a partner at KPMB Architects whose work is defined by a rigorous approach to design, materiality, and craft. Since joining the firm in 1995, he has played a leading role in shaping many of KPMB's most recognized projects across cultural, academic, workplace, residential, and civic sectors. His expertise spans the full lifecycle of a project, combining strong design leadership with technical precision to ensure architectural vision is carried through construction. His portfolio includes the Governor General's Medal-winning Remai Modern, the RIBA Award-winning Gardiner Museum renewal, the Rotman School of Management Expansion, and Boston University's Duan Family Center for Computing & Data Sciences, one of the largest fossil fuel-free buildings in the Boston area. He is currently leading the design of the Montreal Holocaust Museum and the Vancouver Art Gallery.
