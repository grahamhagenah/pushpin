Before Salem: Witchcraft in Boston’s North End, 1688: Cotton Mather, Martha Goodwin, and the Making of a Witchcraft Panic

North End Library · Wed, Nov 18 · 6:30pm

Join the Friends of the North End Branch Library in welcoming James Pasto for Before Salem: Witchcraft in Boston’s North End, 1688: Cotton Mather, Martha Goodwin, and the Making of a Witchcraft Panic.

In 1688, Boston’s North End was the scene of one of New England’s most famous witchcraft cases. Four children of the Goodwin family were believed to have been bewitched, and Cotton Mather, one of Boston’s leading Puritan ministers, became closely involved in the case. This talk examines the Goodwin case within the broader history of Puritan Boston and the beliefs, fears, and social tensions that shaped accusations of witchcraft. Drawing on insights from cultural anthropology, it explores how witchcraft beliefs function in different societies, why accusations arise, and how the events in Boston helped set the stage for the Salem witchcraft trials of 1692.

Presented by James S. Pasto, Ph.D.
