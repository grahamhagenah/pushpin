Behind the Screen: Once Upon a Time in Harlem

West Newton Cinema · Thu, Nov 19 · 6:30pm

ONCE UPON A TIME IN HARLEM captures an extraordinary evening in 1972, when pioneering documentarian William Greaves gathered surviving luminaries of the Harlem Renaissance at Duke Ellington's Harlem townhouse. Over four hours, artists, writers, and intellectuals, many of whom had not seen one another in fifty years, reminisced, argued, laughed, and debated their place in a rapidly changing cultural landscape. Greaves considered it the most important event he ever filmed. Decades later, his son David Greaves, one of the original camera operators, restored the 16mm footage and completed the film, creating both a vivid time capsule and a searching inquiry into one of the most creative periods in American history.

Our panelists will discuss the Harlem Renaissance and its legacy, the remarkable figures gathered in Greaves's footage, and the story of how this film was made and, fifty years later, finally completed.

Kendra Taira Field is the Gerald R. Gill Associate Professor of History and Director of the Center for Public History at Tufts University; and a 2025-26 Hutchins Fellow at Harvard University. A citizen of the Muscogee (Creek) Nation, Field is the author of Growing Up with the Country (Yale, 2018), which traced her own ancestors’ post emancipation lives in Indian Territory and Oklahoma. Field’s American Historical Review article, "The Privilege of Family History" explores the history of African American engagement with family history and genealogy in slavery and freedom. Field abridged David Levering Lewis' W.E.B. Du Bois: A Biography (Henry Holt, 2009) and co-founded the Du Bois Forum, a retreat for writers, scholars, and artists. Field serves as Chief Historian for the 10 Million Names Project and has appeared in numerous historical documentaries and television programs, including Henry Louis Gates, Jr.'s "The African Americans: Many Rivers to Cross" (2013) and HBO Max’s, Breaking New Ground

(2024).
