Author Talk: Cotten Seiler on “White Care”

Boston Public Library · Thu, Dec 10 · 12pm

Join Dickinson College professor Cotten Seiler for a conversation about his new book, White Care. The book discusses how few of us, going about our daily lives, notice the infrastructure that surrounds us and makes those lives possible. What also remains hidden in plain sight is how public infrastructure in the United States has been shaped by theories of race and human evolution. Cotten Seiler’s White Care shows that, from the late 1800s to the present, expert assessments of the quality of the national population have both driven and thwarted the state’s provision of infrastructures of water, sanitation, energy, transportation, recreation, and education. If a nation’s infrastructure is a concrete measure of its belief in its population’s future, what does the current state of American infrastructure say about us?

Lunch will be served and registration is required to attend.

Cotten Seiler is a professor of American Studies at Dickinson College whose work focuses on U.S. cultural and intellectual history, race, feminist and political theory, and infrastructure humanities. He is the author of the books Republic of Drivers: A Cultural History of Automobility in America (Chicago, 2008) and White Care: The Impact of Race on American Infrastructure (Chicago, 2026), coeditor of Engaging Mobilities: Interventions in Pedagogy and Practice (Berghahn, forthcoming 2027), and editor-in-chief of Transfers: Interdisciplinary Journal of Mobility Studies. His essays have appeared in journals such as American Quarterly, Public Culture, Social Text, Reviews in American History, and History & Technology. Crockett's research focuses on large-scale land use changes in twentieth century American cities and examines the social and geographic implications of structural poverty, racial formations and memory. Her book, People before Highways: Boston Activists, Urban Planners, and a New Movement for City Making (UMass Press 2018) investigates a 1960s era grassroots movement to halt urban extension of the U.S. interstate highway system and the geographic and political changes in Boston that resulted.
