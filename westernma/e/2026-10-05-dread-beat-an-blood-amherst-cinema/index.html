Dread Beat An Blood

Amherst Cinema · Mon, Oct 5 · 7pm

“The regal dub poet” (Anderson Tepper, The New York Times) Linton Kwesi Johnson (LKJ) is a living legend. Born in 1952 in Chapelton, a small town in rural Jamaica, he moved to Britain as a boy in 1963, joined the British Black Panthers in 1970, and became the voice for British African-Caribbean young people, creating a genre called dub poetry: militant passionate verse about the Black liberation struggle in Britain set to heavy dub.

DREAD BEAT AN BLOOD (“one of the most powerful examples of the pervasive influence that Jamaican and reggae culture had on 1970s England” —Ink 19) by Franco Rosso, director of the incendiary BABYLON, is a potent portrait of LKJ and his fervent verse and robust voice, with his pen and his microphone.

Now presented in a beautiful new restoration from the original 16mm elements by the British Film Institute never before seen in North America, the film kinetically documents LKJ’s live poetry and reggae performances; his recording and mixing sessions with producer Dennis Bovell (a reggae legend in his own right); and his fervent activism, including a protest for the unjustly imprisoned George Lindo.

dir. Roger Thomas | 26 min | 1985 | NR Screening directly following DREAD BEAT AN BLOOD is this rarely-shown 1985 performance, featuring Johnson with the Dennis Bovell Dub Band (the backing group on his seminal 1984 album, Making History), filmed for Channel 4 in the UK.
