HUG: Devendra Banhart

Mass MoCA · Fri, Oct 9 · 8pm · $46–$76

Witness a special musical collaboration in action as the trio behind their self-titled album Hug — Devendra Banhart, Gyan Riley and Noah Georgeson — debut their deeply intuitive, creative partnership on stage featuring abstract films of Harry Smith Archives.

The three musicians who wrote and recorded Hug — Banhart, Riley and Georgeson — are not related by blood, but they sure act like it. Whatever it is that connects these three, you can hear it in their banter as much as you can in their music. The record is almost like a bait and switch: You read the spine and — especially if you’re familiar with the work of Devendra or Gyan or Noah — you think, this is a guitar trio. But Hug doesn’t particularly sound like any of these artists, and if it’s a guitar trio, then it’s one without too many guitars. Hug is more than the sum of its parts — because these artists know how to play with each other.

“An excellent display of both musicianship and friendship.” – Pitchfork “Soothing, minimalist, and adventurous.” – All Music HUG’s performance at MASS MoCA is also one of only four on the debut U.S. tour to feature abstract films of Harry Smith Archives. Harry Smith is a singular figure of the American avant-garde who moved fluidly between disciplines, assembling a body of work that resists containment—from visionary abstract cinema to his groundbreaking Anthology of American Folk Music. Deeply engaged with mysticism, folklore, and systems of hidden knowledge, Smith approached art as a form of spiritual inquiry—where sound, image, and pattern become tools for re-enchanting the world. Over the course of fifty years he made innovative use as an outsider to recording and distribution technologies, becoming a pioneer of collecting, organizing, and sequencing images and artifacts that structure the ways we share culture today. He created a life and practice outside of institutions and capitalism.

Hug members Gyan Riley and Noah Georgeson grew up together in the New Age enclave of Nevada City, California, bonding over a shared obsession with classical guitar before their musical paths led them to influential careers in experimental, folk, and contemporary music. Years later, they met Devendra Banhart, whose similarly unconventional upbringing and early devotion to music made him a natural creative counterpart.
