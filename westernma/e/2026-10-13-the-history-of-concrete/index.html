The History of Concrete

2 theaters (Amherst Cinema, Images Cinema) · Tue, Oct 13 · from 2pm

Following the finale of his TV series, filmmaker John Wilson looks to the bedrock of his beloved New York City for inspiration and structure.

Concrete, the world's most widely used building material, appears as an ever-present life force, and John sets out to make the definitive documentary on the matter.

To tell this seemingly drab story, he decides to use the industry-approved Hallmark formula to increase his movie's appeal.

But his foundation soon begins to crumble—literally and figuratively—as he struggles to balance artistic freedom with the realities of the modern film industry.

“This is a weird, fun movie that goes in some very unexpected directions... You’re left wondering how a movie about concrete could leave a lump in your throat. But that’s how Wilson works, making us see what we might not have otherwise.” Alissa Wilkinson, New York Times Documentary Lens “For all the earnest substance built into the arch absurdities of THE HISTORY OF CONCRETE, Wilson approaches his subject—and, indeed, the world—with aesthetic ardor... his giddy connections are as beautiful as they are humane.”

Directed by John Wilson
