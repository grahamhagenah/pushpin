Post-film Q&A with Victoria Rizo Lenshyn, Associate Director of DEFA Film Library, and Stephen R. Bissette, cartoonist, author, and film critic.

Presented in partnership with the DEFA Film Library at UMass Amherst and the DEFA-Stiftung in Berlin.

In the mid-21st century, the research vessel Ikaros has disappeared in space. Commander Veikko of the spaceship Laika assembles a crew to search for the missing ship.

The mission is anything but routine. As the team travels through the meteoroid belt between Mars and Jupiter, they receive strange radio signals shortly before discovering the empty wreck of the Ikaros. The signals seem to point to the existence of a foreign civilization and the crew is afraid of a potential threat. Commander Veikko, however, is convinced that such a technically proficient civilization must advance beyond hostility and aggression.

SIGNALS: A SPACE ADVENTURE was the first and most ambitious of two epic space adventures that the prolific East German genre director Gottfried Kolditz (1922-1982)—better known for his fairytale adaptations, film operas, and Westerns—made for the state-run DEFA Studio for Feature Films. SIGNALS was DEFA’s attempt from behind the Iron Curtain to outdo Stanley Kubrick’s 2001: A SPACE ODYSSEY. The film used many of the same tricks: expansive, visually stunning shots of the cosmos and gorgeous Futurist space-design and costumes.

Originally filmed in 70mm cinematography, the film was recently restored in 6K from the original camera negative by the DEFA Foundation in Berlin.

Stephen R. Bissette is an internationally recognized cartoonist and was recently named Vermont’s Cartoonist Laureate (2026-29). He is best known for his collaboration with writer Alan Moore and inker John Totleben on Saga of the Swamp Thing with DC Comics from 1983-87. He is the founding faculty member at The Center for Cartoon Studies (CCS) in White River Junction and has published short stories, novellas, and co-authored and illustrated comic books.
