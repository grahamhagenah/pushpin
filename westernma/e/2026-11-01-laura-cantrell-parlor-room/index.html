Laura Cantrell

Parlor Room · Sun, Nov 1 · 7:30pm · $24

On July 17th, Propeller Sound Recordings will rerelease country music artist Laura Cantrell’s Humming By The Flowered Vine, originally released in 2005 on Matador Records. The “Humming album,” as it became known to Cantrell fans, is a pivotal work in Cantrell’s music career – her previous recordings Not The Tremblin’ Kind and When the Roses Bloom Again had been released in the UK by Glasgow-based indie label Spit and Polish/Shoeshine; in the US, they had been the first full-length, single artist releases on Brooklyn’s Diesel Only Records, run by Cantrell’s husband Jeremy Tepper, and they’d established Cantrell as an Americana talent on the rise. “With my first two albums, we’d had an intense five years – starting with Tremblin’ Kind’s release in January 2000, from our first appearance at Celtic Connections in Glasgow that year, we sprinted nonstop – touring all over the UK and Ireland, earning the support and friendship of BBC legends John Peel and Bob Harris, opening for Willie Nelson and Elvis Costello in the US, playing every kind of gig from local bars all the way to the Grand Ole Opry, it was a terrific blur of hard work and happy accidents,” Cantrell recalls.

“We were thrilled when the opportunity came to work with Matador. Making ‘Humming’ represented a creative leap, working with producer JD Foster and engineer Andy Taub at Brooklyn Recording. Having recorded my previous albums with my gigging band, largely in home studios, these sessions were expansive, with additional musicians like Dave Schramm, Joey Burns and Jon Convertino from Calexico, Amy Helm and Fiona McBain of Ollabelle. As the musical possibilities and sonic palette bloomed, I hoped my songs and voice would hold up in new surroundings. When I felt challenged or unsure, Jeremy was confident and excited, and his enthusiasm kept me steady through the process.”

The Humming By The Flowered Vine reissue features two songs by Dave Schramm, “And Still” and bonus track “Hammer and Nails.” Cantrell recalls, “I’d loved Dave’s ‘Hammer and Nails’ since he’d released his acoustic album with that tune as a title track a few years earlier, and I’d played it a fair amount on Radio Thrift Shop. I remember in the recording studio being really proud of our version, then realized as we finalized the track list we’d need to leave it off the album for time.
