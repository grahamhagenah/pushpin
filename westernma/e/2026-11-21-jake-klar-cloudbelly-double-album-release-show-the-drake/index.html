$18 advanced, $22 day of show General Admission (standing + limited seating) - Doors open at 7PM JAKE KLAR & BLUE RANGER // YEARS GO THROUGH ME NOW Years Go Through Me Now, a new collaborative album by singer-songwriter Jake Klar and folk-rock trio Blue Ranger, is Klar’s first full-length since his 2017 debut, Until the Wild Fire Becomes Paradise — “No one ever slapped my hand for being too fast,” he smiles — with the interceding years seeing a steady stream of singles and EPs (including a pair of beautifully bare EPs, Witness and Masquerade). In its nine songs and two interludes, I hear the motions of weathered hands, the gentle rush of country roads, the laughter of old friends and the silence of rumination — the quilt of dreams and doings we call a life.

GoldFlakePaint describes Klar’s music as “An urgent meeting of folk, roots and good old rock and roll that carries with it an unbreakable spirit.” A fixture in New England’s folk scene, Klar has toured nationally, played festivals including Otis Mountain Get Down and FreshGrass, and supported the likes of Cut Worms, Antwaun Stanley, and Al Olender.

Klar has spent the past decade working as a letterpress printer, art store clerk, bartender, carpenter, illustrator, graphic designer, musician (touring DIY on his own and as a member of Prewn and Hannah Mohan), live sound engineer, merch guy, and custodian. His songwriting, from the misty-eyed title track to the down-home boogie-woogie of “Driving Home Through the Country” (here recast murmurously from an earlier electric version), dwells in a rustic realm borne by his crackling voice and hearthside acoustic guitar. “That Forever” is a haunted, fragmented road-movie that emerges from its dust like a lost Basement Tapes recording, while the last-call piano ballad “Louisa” wouldn’t sound amiss on After The Gold Rush.

The collaboration proved intuitive and ideal; painterly masters of a bittersweet Americana, the trio lent Klar’s songs their most mature and poignant accompaniment yet. Matt Griffin’s hazy drums seem to emerge from memory or wishful thinking, while brothers Josh and Evan Marré contribute a range of instruments, including bass, pedal steel, synthesizers, and sublime slide guitar.
