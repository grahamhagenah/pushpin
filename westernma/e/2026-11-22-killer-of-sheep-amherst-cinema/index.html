Join us for a free screening of KILLER OF SHEEP as part of our 20th Anniversary Kickoff, presenting three films from Amherst Cinema's first year. Free tickets are available the day of the celebration on a first come, first served basis. Arrive early for treats and games!

KILLER OF SHEEP originally played at Amherst Cinema on October 25, 2007, with director Charles Burnett in person.

In the Los Angeles neighborhood of Watts, Stan, employed at a slaughterhouse, is suffering from the emotional side effects of his bloody occupation to such a degree that his entire life unhinges.

One of the crown jewels of the Black indie filmmaking movement known as the L.A. Rebellion, KILLER OF SHEEP focuses on everyday life in Black communities in a manner rarely seen in American cinema—combining lyrical elements with a starkly neorealist, documentary-style approach that chronicles the unfolding story with depth and riveting simplicity.

The film has been declared a national treasure by the Library of Congress and was among the first 50 films placed on the National Film Registry. In 2022, it was named the 43rd greatest film of all time on the British Film Institute Sight and Sound poll.

KILLER OF SHEEP is presented in a new 4K restoration.

“Shot in Watts in the early ’70s and completed in 1977, the film was Mr. Burnett’s M.F.A. thesis project and never meant for a commercial release. He operated the 16-millimeter camera himself, edited the black-and-white images into a visual poem and added the ballads, the jazz and the moody blues that seep into your head like smoke. The result is an American masterpiece, independent to the bone.” Manohla Dargis. A New York Times Critic's Pick!
