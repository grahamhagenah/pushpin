$15 advanced, $20 at the door General Admission (Seated) - Doors open at 7PM Amber Wolfe is an astrologer, Waldorf teacher, and musician. Amber performs solo, with Zizia, and the duo, Ganesh and Wolfe. Her forthcoming album, Half of Dust, will be released in November 2026 by Sedimental and Feeding Tube records.

Amber has been part of the ecological sound art group Zizia since 2018. She and Jarrod Fowler collaborated with Michael Pisaro-Liu as the group Pisaura, which released Asteraceae (Sedimental, 2020). Zizia’s album, Genera (hibari, 2023) includes sonic contributions from 36 other musicians. Recently, Amber has begun performing with improviser Ananya Ganesh as Ganesh & Wolfe.

As Amber Wolfe, she composes and performs folk songs with guitar and voice. Her familiarity with mythology, folklore, and fairytales weave throughout her lyrics, which reflect her connection to the cycles of the natural world. Amber accompanies herself with minimalist guitar, often in nonstandard tunings derived from ancient associations between the planets and musical scales.

Wolfe’s compositions often reflect the practice of astrological indeterminacy, whereby she selects tones that reflect the position of the planets at the time of a concert or recording. Her masters thesis, Tone Art: Astrology, Anthroposophy, Music details her approach to astrological indeterminacy and its connection to the philosophy of Rudolf Steiner, founder of Waldorf Education.

Amber was born and raised in Takoma Park, Maryland, lived for many years in Massachusetts’ Connecticut River Valley, and now resides on the New Hampshire seacoast.

Beth Kaplan founded the Boston band Salem 66 with guitarist Judy Grunwald and drummer Susan Merriam in 1981. Known for haunting vocals and melodic bass lines, Salem 66 released five records on New York's Homestead label before breaking up in 1990.

In 2025, Don Giovanni Records remastered the long out-of-print original recordings and released them for the first time on the streaming services along with SALT, a new vinyl /CD compilation, featuring liner notes by Franklin Bruno, essays from the two songwriters, and a slew of previously unpublished photos. As Rolling Stone put it, "lost 1980s Indie rock pioneers Salem 66 are getting their moment in the sun."
