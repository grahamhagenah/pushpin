The Either/Orchestra plays Éthiopiques

The Drake · Thu, Jan 14 · 8pm · $35–$40

General Admission (Seated w/ Standing / Dancing) - Doors at 7PM $35 Advance, $40 Day of Show The Either/Orchestra plays Ethiopiques!

Éthiopiques is the gold standard for the way archival reissues can rewrite a country’s place in music history. It’s a triumphant story, and its most influential North American collaborators, the Either/Orchestra, have played an important hand in it as the only non-Ethiopian group featured in the series.

Their breakthrough 2005 release, Ethioiques 20: “Live in Addis,” was described as "Astonishing...monumental...the best live album of the year in any genre" (Paul Olsen, On their most recent release, Ethiopiques 32, the 10-piece orchestra plays the music of Nerses Nalbandian, the Armenian-Ethiopian refugee who did much to modernize his adopted country’s music as music director of the Haile Selassie National Theater.

For their first appearance at the Drake, the E/O will be joined by vocalists Munit Mesfin and Bruck Tesfaye for songs from both albums, as well as Ethiopian classics the E/O learned by touring with old school greats including Mahmoud Ahmed and Mulatu Astatke.

Founded in Cambridge, Massachusetts in 1985 by saxophonist and composer Russ Gershon, the ten-piece Either/Orchestra has carved out a singular place in jazz history with its adventurous blend of tradition, experimentation, and global collaboration. Drawing equally from bebop, avant-garde, and punk DIY spirit, the ensemble has served as a proving ground for future stars like John Medeski, Matt Wilson, Miguel Zenón, and Jaleel Shaw, while releasing a dozen acclaimed albums including the Grammy-nominated Calculus of Pleasure.

Downbeat Magazine called them “the best little big band ever."
